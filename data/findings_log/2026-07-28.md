# 2026-07-28 — Schema v2 migration, rescore, and pipeline overhaul (manual run)

Not a normal research run. This was a structural fix to the pipeline itself, triggered by an audit of how the tracker identifies, scores, categorizes, summarizes, and presents research.

## Problems found in the audit

1. **Local files had forked from the published catalog.** Local `opportunities.json` was at 2026-07-08 with 20 opportunities; the published artifact was at 2026-07-27 with 25. Sixteen shared opportunities had drifted. Cause: scheduled cloud runs cannot reach this filesystem, so `AGENT_PROMPT.md` §5 (write the audit trail) never executed. `findings_log/` had exactly one entry. The published page had silently become the sole system of record.
2. **`rubric.md` had stopped governing scheduled runs.** Cloud runs read the *embedded* `rubric-snapshot`, which was a lossy summary — it dropped the conflict-of-interest adjustment, the tier scoring notes (MGI/BHI → 4, analyst → 2 without methodology), and all of §8 dedup guidance. Human edits to `rubric.md` never reached the automation.
3. **Score inflation via a one-way ratchet.** 22 of 25 opportunities sat at 4–5. Between Jul 8 and Jul 27 essentially every changed score went *up*; none went down. Evidence only ever accumulated and there was no downgrade path.
4. **"Independent corroboration" was counted by evidence rows, not institutions.** `ai-bifurcates-it-staffing` scored 5 on 7 rows spanning only 3 institutions (BLS ×5, three of them the same Aug-28 OOH release). `middle-management-overload` scored 4 on 5 rows from 2 institutions (HBR ×4). `it-io-teams` scored 5 on 4 rows from 2 institutions (Gartner ×3).
5. **The evidence ladder didn't fit the domain.** Level 5 demanded "multiple large-enterprise case studies with quantified outcomes"; not one score-5 trail contained a true case study. The actual best evidence was BLS projections and an NBER matched event-study — excellent work with no slot on the scale, so the agent inflated instead.
6. **Structurally confirmation-biased.** Nothing ever searched for disconfirming evidence.
7. **Presentation gaps.** Search covered only title+summary (so "Gartner", "BLS", "NBER" returned nothing); no source-tier or reliability filter; no URL state; no evidence-freshness signal; no coverage visibility; "Legacy/Declining" sorted as most-mature.

## Changes made

**Rubric (`rubric.md`, rewritten).** Split the single evidence score into two axes: **Evidence Strength** (best study design in the trail) and **Corroboration Breadth** (independent institutions, tier-diverse). Composite = `round_half_up((2 × strength + breadth) / 3)`, weighting design 2:1 — only strength ≥5 with breadth ≥4 can reach a 5. Added the independence rule (count institutions and `studyId`s, never rows), a contradiction downgrade path gated at reliability ≥3, a preprint reliability ceiling of 3, evidence-freshness tracking with a 6-month `stale` flag, company-size applicability, structured `relatedTo` edges, and claim-provenance requirements. The file is now published verbatim as `rubric-snapshot.fullText`.

**Sources (`sources.json`).** 31 → 37 institutions across a new 5th tier. Added Census Bureau BTOS (flagged priority — the only large-sample firm-level measurement of *actual* US AI adoption, vs. the catalog's heavy weighting toward stated intentions), Dallas/Atlanta/Richmond Feds, MIT CISR (flagged priority — the research center dedicated to exactly this topic), SIM, and a preprint tier (SSRN, arXiv) with a hard reliability cap of 3. Added per-institution `tracking` blocks (`lastSearched`, `lastYield`, `consecutiveEmptyRuns`) so a silently-dead source becomes visible. Published verbatim as `sources-snapshot.fullSource`.

**Catalog (schema v2).** All 25 opportunities migrated and rescored from their existing evidence — no new research needed for the rescore. Every evidence row gained `institution`, `studyId`, and `peerReviewed`. Added `evidenceStrength`, `evidenceStrengthRationale` (auditable per-opportunity justification), `corroborationBreadth`, `breadthBasis`, `previousEvidenceScore`, `contested`, `contradictingEvidence[]`, `lastRedTeamedDate`, `companySize`, `newestEvidenceDate`, `stale`, `relatedTo` (symmetric), and `metrics[]` — 55 quantified claims extracted with `studyId` provenance links, all resolving.

**Score movements: 15 down, 1 up, 9 unchanged.**

Largest corrections — `ai-infrastructure-network-skills-gap` 5→3 and `it-io-teams-adopt-ai-cost-optimization` 5→3 (both were Gartner/Deloitte row-count inflation across 2–3 institutions); `ceo-mandate-ai-operational-capability-overhaul` 4→2 (a single Gartner survey). The three that held at 5 earned it: `genai-adoption-plateau-scaling-gap` (OECD + Eurostat official statistics + Census BTOS via the Fed, 9 institutions, 3 tiers), `ai-bifurcates-it-staffing` (BLS national projections), `coding-productivity-gains-attenuate-at-shipping` (NBER matched event-study of 100,000+ developers).

**Red-team pass — 11 opportunities at score ≥4 challenged.**
- **1 genuine contradiction found.** Deloitte, *Cutting roles creates budget, not value* (2026-06-17): "over 80% of companies deploying autonomous business capabilities are also reducing their workforce" — directly against `ai-productivity-reallocated-not-cut`, whose central EY figure is 17% reducing headcount. Deloitte discloses no methodology or sample, so it scores reliability 2 and per §4e records as contradicting and flags the opportunity `contested` **without** moving the score. (This gating rule was added mid-pass, precisely so a no-methodology opinion piece can't knock a point off an eight-institution finding — the old problem with the sign flipped.)
- **1 internal contradiction promoted.** `entry-level-hiring-contraction-mixed-evidence` was carrying a NY Fed job-postings analysis finding that AI-exposed posting declines *predate ChatGPT* and that junior and senior roles decline in parallel — evidence cutting against its own thesis, filed as support. Moved to `contradictingEvidence[]`; at reliability 5 it triggers a real −1, holding the opportunity at 4 and marking it contested.
- **1 corroboration found.** arXiv preprint 2605.23135 (2026-05-22), a longitudinal matched cohort of 95 engineers, independently reproduces the attenuation-and-supervision pattern in `coding-productivity-gains-attenuate-at-shipping`. Added as supporting evidence at reliability 3 (preprint ceiling), lifting breadth to 5.
- **The other 8 theses survived.** Searches for disconfirmation on IT support employment and enterprise AI value realization returned corroboration, not contradiction. All 11 carry `lastRedTeamedDate` so "survived a search" stays distinguishable from "nobody looked."

**Page (`site/artifact.html`, rebuilt from a new `template.html` + `build.py`).** Search now covers shortSummary, evidence text, source names and institutions. Added source-tier and reliability filters, contested/stale toggles, full URL state (shareable filtered views), CSV export, two-axis score display with `breadthBasis`, contested/stale/preprint badges, contradicting-evidence sections, related-opportunity links, metric tiles, and evidence-freshness dates. Fixed Legacy/Declining sorting. Added two collapsible panels: a **role × dimension coverage heatmap** (blind-spot finder) and an **evidence timeline** (one row per opportunity, dots by publication date, shaded by reliability). Both use lightness-monotonic sequential ramps validated in light and dark; the categorical palette I first tried failed four of the six colour checks and was dropped in favour of encoding magnitude, which is what these actually measure. Retained the earlier mobile fix.

## Coverage gaps still open

Role tagging remains lopsided: **IT Leadership 17** and **Software Engineering 11** against **Product Management 1**, **QA/Test Engineering 2**, **Data Engineering 2**, **Help Desk/IT Support 2**. 64 of 78 role × dimension cells have evidence. `AGENT_PROMPT.md` now requires every run to aim at least one search at an under-covered role, and explicitly forbids closing gaps by tagging generously.

## Addendum (2026-07-29) — GitHub Pages mirror formalized

The public mirror at https://sdm1130.github.io/ai-it-research-tracker/ had been receiving weekly commits (through 2026-07-27) but was **undocumented** — no step in `AGENT_PROMPT.md` covered it, so it existed only as tribal knowledge and would have been dropped silently by any run that didn't already know about it. Now formalized as §5b.

- Added `site/publish_pages.py`. `site/artifact.html` is a fragment (starts at `<title>`; the Artifact host supplies `<!doctype>`, `<html>`, `<head>` and the viewport meta), so the script wraps it into a standalone document before pushing via the GitHub Contents API. A verbatim `cp` would have rendered in quirks mode with **no viewport tag** — reintroducing exactly the mobile breakage fixed earlier this week. The script refuses to run on an unbuilt template or an already-wrapped document, and no-ops when content is unchanged.
- Added `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` guarded by `:root:where(:not([data-theme]))`. On the standalone copy nothing stamps `data-theme`, so dark mode was previously unreachable there; the guard keeps an explicit stamp (the Artifact host's toggle) winning in both directions.
- **Scope decision: local runs only.** Publishing authenticates through this machine's `gh` keyring, which a scheduled cloud sandbox cannot reach. Cloud runs update the Artifact and must report the mirror as stale; local runs always publish, even on a no-new-findings run, since the mirror may be several runs behind.
- Public-exposure decision: confirmed to publish the page as-is, so the verbatim rubric and source allowlist embedded in it are now on the open web.
- Verified live: 200, 309,772 bytes, schema v2, 25 opportunities, score distribution 5:3 / 4:8 / 3:10 / 2:4, rubric fullText (12,524 chars) intact, doctype + viewport + prefers-color-scheme all present.

## Addendum 2 (2026-07-29) — reverted to single-theme light

Superseding the dark-mode support added in Addendum 1: the page is now **always light**, on both surfaces, by explicit request.

- Removed the `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block and both `:root[data-theme="dark"]` / `[data-theme="light"]` token sets. All dark token values are gone from the stylesheet.
- Added `:root { color-scheme: light !important; }`. The `!important` is load-bearing, not defensive: the Artifact host's frame runtime assigns `documentElement.style.colorScheme` **inline** when a viewer uses its theme toggle, and author `!important` is the only thing in the cascade that beats an inline style. Without it the palette would stay light while native scrollbars, form controls and date pickers rendered dark — reading as a bug rather than a choice.
- Added `<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">` to the Pages wrapper in `publish_pages.py`.
- Documented in `CLAUDE.md` as a do-not-revert decision, since the artifact-design default is to ship both themes and a future run would otherwise "fix" it back.
- Verified on both surfaces: no `prefers-color-scheme` rule, no `data-theme` rule, no dark tokens, single `color-scheme: light !important` declaration. Live Pages build 307,676 bytes.

## Addendum 3 (2026-07-29) — token auth for scheduled Pages publishing

Superseding Addendum 1's "local runs only" scope decision: scheduled cloud runs can now publish the mirror themselves.

- `site/publish_pages.py` rewritten with two auth paths, tried in order: a token from `GH_TOKEN`/`GITHUB_TOKEN` (talks to the REST API directly via `urllib` — the cloud-run path, since a sandbox cannot reach this machine's keyring), then the `gh` CLI (local runs). The token value is never printed and never has to pass through a chat transcript.
- **Required PAT permission is exactly one thing:** fine-grained, repository access limited to `sdm1130/ai-it-research-tracker`, `Contents: Read and write`. `Metadata: Read-only` is added automatically by GitHub. Verified nothing else is needed: `main` has no branch protection and no rulesets (checked), and Pages is `build_type: legacy` on `main:/` so it rebuilds automatically on push — no Pages-write, no Actions permission.
- Exit codes are now contractual, because the failure mode that matters is a **silently expired token** rotting the public site for weeks: `0` published/unchanged, `3` no credentials (skip and report the mirror stale — explicitly not an error), `1` real failure (report loudly, quoting the error). `AGENT_PROMPT.md` §5b now instructs the agent to act on each code and to never report the mirror as updated unless the exit was 0.
- Added `--check-auth` to verify credentials and push permission without writing anything.
- Error handling calls out the fine-grained-PAT gotcha specifically: GitHub returns **404, not 403**, for a repo a token isn't scoped to, which otherwise reads as "file missing" rather than "wrong token scope."
- Tested all reachable paths: `gh` auth OK (`sdm1130`, push permission confirmed); no-credentials → exit 3 with the stale-mirror message; invalid token → exit 1 with expiry guidance; unchanged content → exit 0 no-op. The valid-token read/write path could not be exercised without a real token, but it shares URL and payload construction with the `gh` path already confirmed working.

## Addendum 4 (2026-07-29) — scheduled routine rewritten; repo becomes source of truth

Investigating how to add a PAT surfaced two things that mattered more than the PAT.

**1. No PAT is needed, and none should be added.** The trigger declares the repo as a git source (`sources: [{git_repository: {url: .../ai-it-research-tracker}}]`), so the sandbox clones it with push credentials already attached. Confirmed empirically: the weekly commits on `main` for 2026-07-20 and 2026-07-27 were authored by `sdm1130 <sdm1130@users.noreply.github.com>` — the cloud run, not the human — matching the cron (`0 13 * * 1`) and `last_fired_at`. `api_token_hint` on the trigger is empty. A PAT would have added a secret to rotate and an expiry to be bitten by, for nothing. `publish_pages.py` gained a **git path** (preferred over the API precisely because there is no secret involved), tried before token and `gh`.

**2. The routine was a full major version behind, and would have regressed everything.** Its prompt carried the entire v1 process inline: old four-tier allowlist, old single-axis evidence scoring, old opportunity schema, no red-team step, no independence rule, hand-rolled HTML wrapping instructions, and `favicon: 🧭` (which also answered the open question of what the original favicon was — the 🏢 published this week was a guess). Had it fired on 2026-08-03 it would have rewritten `opportunities-snapshot` without `evidenceStrength`, `corroborationBreadth`, `breadthBasis`, `contested`, `metrics`, `relatedTo`, `companySize` or `stale`, degrading the new page to empty pips and no flags, and re-inflating scores on the retired rubric.

**Root cause, and the fix.** Carrying the process inline in the schedule meant the rubric the automation actually scored against could drift from `rubric.md` indefinitely — the same class of failure as the lossy embedded rubric snapshot found earlier this week, one layer up. So:

- Committed the pipeline to the repo: `AGENT_PROMPT.md`, `rubric.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `data/` (catalog, sources, config, findings log), `site/` (template, `build.py`, `publish_pages.py`), plus a `README.md` and `.gitignore` (build outputs and local backups excluded). Secret-scanned before pushing — note the first scan silently no-opped on a `git grep` argument-order error and reported "clean" without running; re-run correctly before trusting it.
- Rewrote the trigger prompt from ~14,000 characters of inline process to ~4,000 that say: clone, read `AGENT_PROMPT.md`, follow it, run `site/build.py` then `site/publish_pages.py`, act on the exit code, publish the Artifact with `favicon: 🏢`. Cron, environment and git source unchanged; added `Edit`/`Glob`/`Grep` to `allowed_tools` since it now works with repo files rather than a scraped HTML string.
- The catalog now lives in git, so a run no longer needs to scrape prior state out of the published page.

**Verified end to end from a fresh clone:** `site/build.py` regenerates the page and the rebuilt-then-wrapped output is **byte-identical** to the committed `index.html` (reproducible build); `publish_pages.py` correctly detects git mode in the clone and `gh` mode on this machine.

**Known consequence:** `/Users/steve/Claude/ai_research` is not a clone, so it will drift from the repo whenever a cloud run commits. Pull `data/` and `rubric.md` from the repo before local work. Making the local directory a clone would remove this class of drift entirely.
