Targeted disconfirmation searches this run and what they found:

- **AI skills wage premium** (`ai-skills-wage-premium-it-developers`, score 5 — the catalog's only score-5 opportunity): searched for evidence the premium is eroding or commoditized. Found an arXiv preprint ("Generative AI impacts on intra-urban inequality and skill premium in Beijing," arXiv:2605.25505) using a difference-in-differences design around ChatGPT's release on 5M Beijing job postings, finding a wage *penalty*, not premium, in neighborhoods with high GenAI occupational exposure. On inspection this measures a different mechanism than the one this opportunity tracks: geographic/occupational automation *exposure* (jobs AI can replace) versus the individual *credential premium* for workers who possess and market AI skills (PwC, Wharton). A worker can hold a marketable AI-skills premium even in a region or occupation experiencing exposure-driven wage stagnation — these are not the same claim, so this was **not** added to `contradictingEvidence[]` despite clearing the reliability-≥3 preprint-ceiling threshold; forcing it in would have manufactured a downgrade from evidence that doesn't actually test this opportunity's claim. Score unchanged at 5.
